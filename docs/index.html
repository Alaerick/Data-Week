Closing In on 1.5

How playstyle's changed in NCAA women's basketball from 2014 to 2026

NCAA Division I women's basketball · 2014–2026

Over the course of the 2014-2026 seasons, teams have increased the number of three point attempts relative to two point attempts. Making three point shot attempts more common, decreasing accuracy on three point shots overall. Meanwhile two point shots have been attempted less, making their overall accuracy higher over time.

It might seem counterintuitive how teams would increase three point attempts while their accuracy on threes declines, but the overall strategy is to maximize points per shot attempt.

Balancing two point shots and three point shots

In basketball, there are two types of shots: twos and threes. A three counts for 3 points and a two for 2, so a three only has to go in two-thirds as often to be worth the same. Take a team's 2-point accuracy (how often its twos go in) and divide it by its 3-point accuracy (how often its threes go in). We call this the accuracy ratio. If it is below 1.5, threes are the better shot. Above 1.5, twos are. At exactly 1.5, it doesn't matter which one you take.

Data & methods

  • Source: the Love Data Week 2026 NCAA Women's Basketball data files (team_box table, one row per team per game).
  • Seasons: 2014–2026. Earlier seasons have too few games to use. The data covers only about 66 teams per season in 2014–2016 and 348+ from 2017 on. The dotted line on each chart marks that change. The original dataset goes from 2006-2026, the data before 2014 is too limited, with as low as only 10 games in 2013, so it's not included.
  • Teams: a team-season counts if the team played at least 20 games. 2-point and 3-point accuracy are totals across all of a team's games (total makes ÷ total attempts), not averages of per-game percentages.
  • Top 10: the ten teams with the best win percentage each season.
  • Limits: the 1.5 rule compares points per attempt only. It ignores free throws and and-ones (more common on twos), offensive rebounds after misses, and shot-clock situations, so the true break-even point for a given team may be a little above or below 1.5.
  • Code: scripts/export_data.py builds the JSON in docs/data/. The charts use D3.
