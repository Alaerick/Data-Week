NCAA Division I women's basketball · 2014–2026

How NCAA teams improved from 2014-2026

A three-pointer is worth 1.5 twos. Over twelve seasons, the game has settled on exactly that price.

Why 1.5?

In basketball, there are two types of shots: twos and threes. A three counts for 3 points and a two for 2, so a three only has to go in two-thirds as often to be worth the same. Divide a team's 2-point percentage by its 3-point percentage: if the result is below 1.5, threes are the better shot. Above 1.5, twos are. At exactly 1.5, it doesn't matter which one you take.

How the price settled

Nobody set the price. Teams took more threes, and the extra threes were harder ones, so 3-point accuracy slipped. With more shooters spread around the arc, the paint opened up and 2-point accuracy rose. Both moves push the ratio toward 1.5.

The twist: teams didn't adjust to their own numbers

If each team went after its own best shot, teams above 1.5 would cut back on threes the next season. They did the opposite. Teams for whom twos were the better shot increased their share of threes the most. The whole league moved together toward the three, and the league average landed on 1.5.

Does balance win?

Somewhat. Teams near 1.5 win a little more often than teams at either extreme.

Data & methods

  • Source: the Love Data Week 2026 NCAA Women's Basketball data files (team_box table, one row per team per game).
  • Seasons: 2014–2026. Earlier seasons have too few games to use. The data covers only about 66 teams per season in 2014–2016 and 348+ from 2017 on. The dotted line on each chart marks that change.
  • Teams: a team-season counts if the team played at least 20 games. 2P% and 3P% are totals across all of a team's games (total makes ÷ total attempts), not averages of per-game percentages.
  • Top 10: the ten teams with the best win percentage each season.
  • Limits: the 1.5 rule compares points per attempt only. It ignores free throws and and-ones (more common on twos), offensive rebounds after misses, and shot-clock situations, so the true break-even point for a given team may be a little above or below 1.5.
  • Code: scripts/export_data.py builds the JSON in docs/data/. The charts use D3.
